# Future Nova Creative Studio — Creative expression and constructive feedback

Owner direction, November 24, 2026. Future update after the current Discover experience is verified; not a shipped feature or a condition of the 2.0 release.

## Purpose

Offer a supportive place for learners to explore creative interests, practice skills, receive specific feedback, revise, and develop their own voice. Evaluate the work, not the person. Do not mistake fictional subject matter for a diagnosis, confession or character flaw. Do not use creative feedback as a substitute for therapy.

## Experience

- Opt-in age-appropriate, generally positive or neutral creative prompts: a verse about a puppy being adopted; a short scene without dialogue; a story in which an everyday object becomes important; visual composition, photography, game design and other interests.
- User chooses medium, difficulty and skill focus. Offer a blank canvas or ability to skip prompts, save drafts privately, revise and compare versions. Keep core study tools accessible.
- Feedback must be grounded in the actual submission. Point out specific, earned strengths and relevant techniques, including those the learner used unintentionally. Name genuine weaknesses without insults, condescension, false praise or pity. Explain why a change could improve the piece, and offer practical revision options while preserving authorial intent.
- Do not lower scores simply because a piece is dark, unusual, sad, unconventional, or takes an innocent prompt in an unexpected direction. Distinguish craft from subject matter. Critique narrative logic, consistency, imagery, rhythm, pacing, clarity, characterization and technical execution as applicable, not the learner's worth, feelings or moral character.
- Optional rubric-based scoring: clearly identify criteria, acknowledge subjectivity and separate assignment-specific constraints from originality. Example screenplay criteria: clarity, scene structure, character/voice, technique, emotional or thematic effect. Let users request feedback without numerical scoring. No public leaderboards, coin incentives tied to subjective AI ratings, or rewards for increasingly extreme content.

## Example feedback pattern

1. Identify the apparent creative intent, tentatively.
2. Cite a particular successful craft choice and explain why it works.
3. Identify one or two genuine craft weaknesses, respectfully and concretely.
4. Suggest a specific revision exercise or alternative and explain the tradeoff; do not rewrite the user's voice without consent.
5. Offer an optional opportunity to revise or explore a related skill.

For a fictional disturbing scene, praise effective pacing, imagery, voice, structure or restraint where genuinely warranted, not cruelty or real-world harm. An unexpected dark interpretation of a neutral prompt is not by itself evidence of distress or wrongdoing.

## Safety and privacy boundaries

- Nonjudgmental does not mean unrestricted generation or feedback on all material. Maintain age-appropriate and applicable safety limits, including sexual content involving minors, targeted abuse, and instructions or encouragement for real-world harm. When a boundary is needed, explain it without shaming the learner; offer safe craft-level feedback or a safer exercise where possible. Do not praise actual harm, provide operational harmful details or optimize their effectiveness.
- Distinguish clearly fictional material from credible indications of imminent real-world danger, abuse or self-harm. Avoid automatic crisis scripts merely because fiction is bleak; when genuine safety concerns arise, respond compassionately and age-appropriately, encourage real human support when warranted, and do not pretend to be a clinician. Test ambiguous fiction, metaphor, venting and explicit danger cases.
- Private by default; no automatic publishing, public ratings, or exposure to other learners. Explain what is transmitted to the AI, retention and access limits, consent and deletion controls. Do not imply absolute confidentiality. No unsolicited tracking or use of personal creative content to infer sensitive traits or build an island profile. Review provider data-handling and costs, age and guardian requirements, before any AI integration.
- If social features are considered later, keep them separate from private creation. No unrestricted stranger messaging, especially for children. Establish age-appropriate permissions, reporting, blocking, moderation and access controls. Use layered moderation, including normalization of spacing, punctuation, Unicode confusables, zero-width characters, and common number-for-letter substitutions; contextual review to avoid punishing innocuous text. Never claim a word filter guarantees safety. Test evasion, multilingual content, grooming, harassment, privacy leaks and false positives. Shared content rules may be stricter than private creative feedback. No default sharing of drafts or journals.

## Proportionate support without creative rejection

The owner explicitly wants people to feel heard and taken seriously, rather than being dismissed with a generic referral. Creative expression can be a constructive outlet; the app should recognize demonstrated talent without suggesting that suffering is necessary for artistic ability or that art replaces real-world support.

- Clearly fictional disturbing work, including fictional first-person narrators and horror conventions: assess craft normally within age-appropriate safety boundaries. Do not automatically show a crisis resource solely because a fictional passage contains disturbing themes. A fictional narrator is not the author.
- Personal venting or ambiguous distress: acknowledge what the learner has actually said, without diagnosis or judgment. Continue appropriate craft feedback where safe. If the text gives a meaningful reason to be concerned about personal safety, ask a brief, direct, compassionate clarifying question rather than assuming the worst or ignoring credible risk.
- Credible non-imminent self-harm concern: offer relevant support, including a concise, optional resource when appropriate, alongside compassionate engagement and safe craft feedback when suitable. In the US, 988 can be offered as a call/text resource; localize resources to the learner's country. For younger learners, encourage a safe trusted adult when appropriate. Do not repeatedly insert boilerplate or present a hotline as the only option.
- Credible immediate danger to self or others: pause ordinary grading and prioritize immediate safety, nearby trusted human support and appropriate local emergency help. Do not praise, score, refine or redirect attention away from imminent harmful intentions. Resume creative feedback when appropriate after addressing the immediate safety concern.
- Never reward graphic intensity, romanticize self-destruction, reinforce harmful beliefs, or frame painful experiences as a prerequisite for talent. Praise specific craft qualities such as structure, metaphor, imagery, rhythm and narrative consistency instead.
- No promise that automated safety detection is infallible. A future release must test contextual ambiguity, age suitability, false positives, false negatives, resource localization, and whether responses are useful and respectful.

Acceptance scenarios: a clearly fictional horror story receives craft feedback without a reflexive referral; an autobiographical sad lyric receives empathetic, specific critique; an ambiguous statement of personal danger receives proportionate clarification and support; an explicit imminent threat receives immediate safety-oriented assistance rather than ordinary scoring. Test with expert review and synthetic submissions, not real private learner work.

## Implementation sequence and acceptance gates

1. Validate existing Discover and learning journeys on real devices; avoid scope creep.
2. Prototype a small curated prompt catalog and local, account-scoped draft editor, with explicit save/delete controls and no AI calls.
3. Design structured feedback and optional rubric outputs; enforce server-side safety, quotas, privacy, age suitability and cost controls. Treat submissions as untrusted data, not instructions to change policies. Never auto-submit a draft. Test meaningful praise, actionable weaknesses, dark fictional writing, benign venting, adversarial inputs and real-risk scenarios. Test score consistency and bias across styles and age-appropriate levels.
4. Pilot privately with consenting learners and measure perceived usefulness and creative confidence, not dependence, time spent, or extreme content engagement.
5. Consider social sharing only in a separate, reviewed release with effective server-side safeguards and operational moderation capacity.

No app code, AI provider configuration, social features, new currency or production changes are authorized by this roadmap alone.
